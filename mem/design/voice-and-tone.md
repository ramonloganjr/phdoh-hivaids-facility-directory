---
name: Voice and tone
description: UX writing rules for HIV care directory — reassurance, dignity, clarity, control
type: design
---
**Audience.** People seeking HIV testing, treatment, or care for themselves or someone they love. Often anxious, sometimes stigmatized, frequently mobile, sometimes on weak connections.

**Primary feelings to evoke:** reassurance & safety · confidence & clarity · warmth & dignity · efficiency & control.

**Voice rules**
- Person-first, never label by status. Say "people living with HIV", never "HIV patients/victims/sufferers".
- Plain language. Short sentences. No medical jargon without a one-line gloss.
- Second person ("you"), warm but not chirpy. No exclamation marks. No emoji in product UI.
- Privacy is stated proactively where the action happens, not buried in /privacy.
- Verbs over nouns in CTAs: "Find care near me", "Get directions", "Open in Maps".
- Errors name the cause + the next step. Never "Oops" / "Something went wrong" alone.
- Empty states are opportunities, not apologies: suggest the next action.
- Numbers are tabular and labeled ("3.2 km away · ~8 min drive").
- Never promise medical outcomes. Never make the user feel watched.

**Forbidden phrasing**
- "Sorry, …", "Oops", "Whoops", "Uh oh"
- "HIV-positive patients", "AIDS sufferers", "high-risk groups"
- "Click here", "Learn more" (use the actual action)

**Preferred phrasing patterns**
- Permission ask: "We'll use your location once to rank facilities by distance. Nothing leaves your device."
- Permission denied: "Location is off. You can still browse the full directory, or enable location in your browser to sort by distance."
- Empty result: "No facilities in this range. Try widening it, or switch service type."
